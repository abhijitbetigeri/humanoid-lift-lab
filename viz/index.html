Gemini 4 Argon hackathon

Humanoid Lift Lab

Humanoids that lift and shift: boxes, stock and furniture parts for small businesses and households, in tight, crowded spaces where getting people to help is hard. Every load is different, and nobody is on site to re-tune the robot. Gemini 4 Argon does that job in simulation: it takes the robot's deployed lift controller, runs it in MuJoCo on boxes from 11 to 88 lb, watches the rendered video of each attempt, explains what went wrong and rewrites the controller for physics to re-test. A hidden physics scorer grades every round; the critic never scores itself.

The goal is to find failures before hardware deployment. This prototype checks controller changes in a modified, assisted humanoid simulation; hardware transfer and held-out evaluation remain future work.

Method and limits

  • Generic humanoid, scaled torques. The body is the Unitree G1 model from MuJoCo Menagerie, with joint torques scaled up to the Unitree H1-2 / Boston Dynamics Atlas class (hip 320 N·m, knee 400 N·m, shoulder 150 N·m). A stock G1 cannot do this task: ResMimic (arXiv 2510.05070) reports a wrist limit of about 2.5 kg. For scale, Atlas is specified at 30 kg sustained and 50 kg brief. So read this as a generic full-size humanoid, not a G1 claim.
  • In-place lift only. The robot squats, grips and stands up with the box. No walking, no carrying, no placing.
  • Physics scores, not the critic. Success comes from the simulator (box rise, hold time, fall, drop, mean balance assist). Argon's critique has no effect on the score.
  • Balance assist. A PD force on the pelvis keeps the early rounds from falling over at once; its mean magnitude is recorded and capped at 60 N for a pass, so a controller cannot lean on it.
  • Result. The deployed controller lifted 22–88 lb but dropped the light 11 lb box. Both arms adapted it to pass all eight loads (11–88 lb) in four rounds: video arm $0.26 of Argon calls, text-only arm $0.18. The video arm made partial progress sooner (3 loads passing at round 2 vs 0), but both ended at 8/8, so with one seed we do not claim video beats text on this task.
  • From scratch is harder. Starting instead from a textbook stoop-lift controller, the video arm ran eight rounds and the text-only arm six (the rest were still running at publish time): the box started rising, but never within the 60 N balance-assist budget, so 0 passes in either arm. Building a balanced whole-body lift from nothing needs more rounds than we ran.
  • Keep-best loop. Both arms start from the same controller. If a rewrite scores worse than the best controller so far (passes first, then partial credit for box rise), Argon is told it was discarded and revises the best one again. The chart shows every round as scored.
  • Solver checks. MuJoCo runs the implicit integrator with a no-slip pass so a held box cannot creep through the grip numerically. Any trial that still went unstable is reported with its physical cause (fell, dropped), never silently passed; videos stop 1.5 s after a fall.
  • Small sample. One seed, one trial per mass, eight rounds per arm. Treat differences of one mass step as noise.
  • Assumed friction. Box μ = 0.8, palm 0.85–1.0, the upper end of rubber on plastic; no published value was found.
  • Related work. NVlabs ASPIRE (arXiv 2607.00272) also has an LLM refine robot skill code in simulation, but for a tabletop arm and without diagnosing physics from video. Payload-robust legged control is usually learned with mass randomization plus RL (RMA, Walk These Ways); here the output is a short controller a person can read.
